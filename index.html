Introducing Kertory-JEPA

A self-supervised
latent world model

Kertory-JEPA is a predictive latent world model that learns reusable physics from observation — tested across three versions, from a fixed-condition failure to few-shot adaptation on the unfamiliar.

The idea in one picture

Why JEPA doesn't predict pixels.

Capabilities

Understand, predict, adapt.

Kertory-JEPA is built around three abilities, tested independently at every version.

01

Understand physical dynamics

A pretrained visual encoder turns raw simulation frames into latent embeddings; a trained predictor learns how those embeddings evolve under gravity, restitution, radius, and friction.

02

Generalize within a range

Once trained across randomized physics, in-range prediction error holds at 5–31% — even with four variables moving at once, instead of memorizing a single fixed condition.

03

Adapt to the unfamiliar

Given just 8 new episodes from a condition it has never seen, the model cuts its own error by 54.6% in 20 adaptation steps — no full retraining required.

The core question

One hypothesis per version. Negative results kept in.

This is the complete research journey across three iterations of Kertory-JEPA — including the version that failed, because it's what motivated everything after it. Every figure below is a measured evaluation result, not a projection.

"Can a self-supervised, JEPA-style predictor learn reusable physics dynamics from observation, well enough to handle physical conditions it was never trained on?"
The journey

From a one-condition failure to a live research roadmap.

V0.1 First prototype REJECTED

Fixed gravity. The model memorized one condition instead of learning a variable.

300 episodes, gravity held constant at 1.0, a 22.3M-parameter predictor trained from scratch on a pretrained visual encoder. Training and validation loss looked healthy — 0.1301 vs 0.1329 — but that only measured performance inside the one condition it had ever seen.

+541%error at unseen gravity = 0.5
+426%error at unseen gravity = 2.0
0variables randomized in training
V0.2 Gravity randomization CONFIRMED

Randomizing gravity during training fixed in-range generalization almost entirely.

Gravity sampled from 0.3–2.5 per episode, 500 episodes, improved temporal pooling and a JEPA-style loss with a per-position mask token. In-range error fell from 541% to 5–31%. Extrapolation beyond the trained range — 85–135% — remained the open problem.

5–31%in-range error increase
85–135%out-of-range error increase
1variable randomized (gravity)
V0.3.1 Multi-variable + few-shot CONFIRMED

Four physics variables at once, plus a test of few-shot adaptation to the unfamiliar.

Gravity, restitution, ball radius, and friction randomized together — 1,000 episodes, 32–35M parameters, five staged tests (A–E). In-range performance held at the same 5–31% band even with four variables moving jointly. Out-of-range error narrowed further to 62–67%. Then Stage E asked a new question entirely.

V0.4 Action-conditioning, real DROID data DID NOT GENERALIZE

Added actions, switched to real robot data — and few-shot adaptation stopped being reliable.

V0.3.1 only predicted what happens next; V0.4's goal was predicting what happens next given an action — the step toward robot control. The synthetic ball simulator was swapped for real Franka-arm data (Hugging Face's muacha/droid-30-converted, 30 episodes, ~437 training windows), with a frozen V-JEPA2 encoder feeding a new 30M-parameter action-conditioned predictor trained for 8-dimensional joint actions.

After 30 epochs, validation loss plateaued at 0.378 — about 20× worse than V0.3.1's 0.019. Few-shot adaptation, tested across six combinations of episode and learning rate, swung between +10.9% and −51.6% with no consistent pattern.

0.378validation loss (vs 0.019 in V0.3.1)
−51.6%worst few-shot result (lr 0.001)
30 / 437episodes / training windows used
The actual live demo (Hugging Face Spaces, Gradio) — this run: 8 few-shot examples, lr 0.00001, result −1.6%. Three likely causes are written up in the Journey notes: too little data (30 episodes vs V0.3.1's 1,000), a training method that tends to learn the average outcome rather than the action's effect, and fine-tuning used in place of real meta-learning.
V0.5 Intervention-effect + meta-learning IN PROGRESS

Back to controlled simulation, starting from V0.3.1's weights — and few-shot adaptation is now trained in, not bolted on.

V0.5 follows one rule: competitors are racing to collect more data; Kertory is racing to learn more from less of it. Three concrete changes from V0.4: (1) back to the controllable KertoryWorld simulator, initialized from V0.3.1's already-trained weights rather than from scratch; (2) intervention-effect supervision — each episode now pairs one starting state with two different actions, so the model is shown directly how the outcome should change with the action, not just one averaged result; (3) Reptile meta-learning folded into training itself — every fourth batch, the model takes a mock few-shot test, adapts in 3 steps, and nudges its real weights toward being faster to adapt, rather than adaptation being a separate step applied after training.

Stage E · the most important result in this series

Few-shot adaptation works.

Zero-shot loss on a fully unseen combination of gravity, restitution, radius, and friction was 0.04057. After a short, low-learning-rate adjustment on just 8 new episodes — 20 steps, not full retraining — loss dropped to 0.01843. That's the first direct evidence that Kertory-JEPA can improve its handling of an unfamiliar environment from a handful of observations, rather than needing to be retrained from zero.

Scope

Stated plainly, so results aren't read more broadly than they support.

Demonstrated

✓
A working, reproducible pipeline

Raw physics simulation → pretrained visual encoder → trained predictor → measured evaluation, unchanged across all three versions.

✓
A diagnosed and directly addressed failure

V0.1's memorization failure was root-caused and fixed by changing the training data, not just the model.

✓
Generalization across four physics variables

Within a trained range, covering gravity, restitution, radius, and friction simultaneously.

✓
A measurable few-shot adaptation effect

A 54.6% error reduction from 8 new observations, without full retraining.

Not demonstrated

✕
Strong extrapolation far outside training

Error still rises substantially there — Stage D, +62–67%.

✕
Physics or causal understanding, formally

No explicit representation of force, mass, or energy conservation.

✕
Any real-world, non-simulated environment

No real object, sensor, or robotics data was used anywhere in this series.

✕
General intelligence, or LLM comparisons

No claim of, or comparison to, general intelligence or language models is made.

Next direction

The golden rule: learn more from less data, not more data than everyone else.

Industry players like Generalist AI and OpenVLA train on 270,000–500,000+ hours of robot data with 7–10B-parameter models. A solo researcher will never out-scale that — so Kertory's bet is fast adaptation from a handful of examples, not scale.

V0.5 — intervention-effect + meta-learning

In progress: paired-action supervision and Reptile meta-learning, trained into the model from the start rather than fine-tuned on afterward.

V0.6 — back to real DROID data

Planned: the same method, applied to 100+ real robot episodes, this time with meta-learning included from the beginning.

V0.7 — permanent demo + physical test

Planned: a permanent Hugging Face Spaces demo, a small real robot-arm test outside simulation, and multi-object scenes.

Across V0.1 to V0.3.1, prediction error under distribution shift fell from +541% to +62–67%, and few-shot adaptation was demonstrated for the first time in this project. V0.4 then showed that adding actions and switching to real robot data isn't a small step — few-shot adaptation stopped being reliable, which is exactly what V0.5's intervention-effect and meta-learning changes are built to fix.

V0.1 through V0.4 are measured, real results. V0.5 is running now. V0.6–V0.7 are the plan, not yet started →
